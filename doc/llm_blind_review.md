## 核心 0: Clodsire + Corviknight + Dondozo + Gliscor + Toxapex

**A. Bulky Stall Wall**  (tags: stall, balance, hazard_stack, setup)

- Game plan: The core blunts physical and special attackers with a wall of Poison-, Steel-, Ground- and Water-type defensive Pokemon. Clodsire and Gliscor set Stealth Rock and Spikes, while Toxapex adds Toxic Spikes and Toxic to wear opponents down. Corviknight uses Defog to clear hazards and Roost to recover. Dondozo (Curse with Rest and Sleep Talk) or Gliscor (Swords Dance with Toxic Orb Poison Heal) can eventually set up and finish a weakened team.
- Roles: Clodsire: Hazard setter and physical wall; Corviknight: Defogger and Steel/Flying pivot; Dondozo: Curse setup win condition and special wall; Gliscor: Hazard setter and Toxic Orb Poison Heal wall; Toxapex: Toxic stalling Haze wall
- Win conditions: ['Dondozo', 'Gliscor']
- Weaknesses: Weak to strong special attackers and Pokemon that threaten multiple members, such as Grass-types like Gouging Fire-style Fire threats and Water/Grass coverage. Steel-types and Poison-types are not hit hard by Toxic, and Taunt or status absorbers like Gholdengo shut down hazards and Toxic. With limited offensive pressure, the team can be worn down by Knock Off and passive recovery loops.
- How to play against: Use Taunt, Knock Off, and strong special attackers like Fire-, Grass-, or Ground-type threats to break through the walls. Bring Poison and Steel immune Pokemon, like Gholdengo, to reflect hazards and ignore Toxic. Set up your own boosters such as Calm Mind or Dragon Dance after removing Haze users.

**B. Fat Stall Hazard Stack**  (tags: ['stall', 'hazard_stack', 'setup'])

- Game plan: The core aims to completely exhaust the opponent's resources through persistent entry hazards, passive damage, and reliable recovery. Clodsire, Gliscor, and Toxapex deploy Spikes, Stealth Rock, and Toxic Spikes while chipping opponents with Toxic, Infestation, and Knock Off. Defensive pivots like Corviknight clear opposing hazards with Defog and pivot with U-turn, while setup variants of Dondozo with Curse, Corviknight with Bulk Up or Iron Defense, and Gliscor with Swords Dance act as late-game win conditions.
- Roles: Clodsire: Special wall and hazard setter; Corviknight: Defogger, physical wall, and setup sweeper; Dondozo: Physical tank, wall, and Curse win condition; Gliscor: Poison Heal staller, hazard setter, and setup sweeper; Toxapex: Mixed wall, hazard setter, and status spreader
- Win conditions: ['Dondozo', 'Corviknight', 'Gliscor']
- Weaknesses: Strong wallbreakers with Taunt or powerful mixed and wall-breaking coverage can dismantle the defensive network. Powerful Electric-, Psychic-, and Freeze-Dry attackers can exploit specific defensive holes if positioning slips.
- How to play against: Prevent their entry hazards early with aggressive pressure and Taunt users. Utilize strong wallbreakers that do not mind passive status or utilize Trick with Choice items to cripple vital defensive walls like Dondozo or Corviknight.

**C. Stall Hazard Stack**  (tags: stall, hazard_stack)

- Game plan: This core wins by blanking opposing threats with overlapping defensive walls, setting Stealth Rock, Spikes, and Toxic Spikes, and removing opposing hazards with Corviknight's Defog. Clodsire, Gliscor, and Toxapex spread poison and chip damage while Recover, Roost, and Rest keep the core healthy. Dondozo's Curse or Gliscor's Swords Dance can close out games once the opponent is worn down, and Corviknight's Bulk Up or Iron Defense Body Press provides another win path.
- Roles: Clodsire: Stealth Rock/Spikes setter, Recover wall, Amnesia win condition; Corviknight: Defogger, physical wall, U-turn pivot, Bulk Up/Iron Defense Body Press; Dondozo: Curse Rest physical wall and win condition; Gliscor: Poison Heal pivot, hazard setter, Toxic spreader, Swords Dance breaker; Toxapex: Toxic/Toxic Spikes spreader, Haze, Recover wall, Infestation trapper
- Win conditions: ['Dondozo', 'Gliscor', 'Corviknight']
- Weaknesses: This core is passive and can be overwhelmed by strong special wallbreakers, Taunt users, and Knock Off removing Leftovers, Heavy-Duty Boots, or Toxic Orb. Gholdengo can block Corviknight's Defog, letting opposing hazard stack chip the team. Setup sweepers that can boost past Haze or force Dondozo to Rest can also break through.
- How to play against: Use Taunt and Knock Off to deny recovery and hazard control, and bring strong special attackers that target Clodsire, Toxapex, or Dondozo's weaker side. Gholdengo or other Good as Gold users can prevent Defog, while hazard stack and Toxic immunity pressure the core. Force Dondozo to use Rest, then attack it during Sleep Talk turns, and use Encore or setup to exploit passive turns.

## 核心 1: Great Tusk + Hydrapple + Jolteon + Kingambit + Moltres + Slowking-Galar

**A. Bulky Balance with Setup Finishers**  (tags: ['balance', 'bulky_offense', 'setup', 'volturn'])

- Game plan: Great Tusk sets Stealth Rock and uses Rocky Helmet to punish contact, while Slowking-Galar with Assault Vest and Moltres with Will-O-Wisp provide special and physical cushioning. Jolteon pivots with Volt Switch and can build with Calm Mind, and Hydrapple can set up with Nasty Plot. Once the checks are worn down, Kingambit with Swords Dance and Supreme Overlord, or a boosted Hydrapple or Jolteon, closes out the game.
- Roles: Great Tusk: Hazard setter and physical tank; Hydrapple: Nasty Plot special sweeper; Jolteon: Volt Switch pivot and Calm Mind attacker; Kingambit: Late-game Swords Dance cleaner; Moltres: Burner and defensive Roost flier; Slowking-Galar: Assault Vest special wall with coverage
- Win conditions: ['Kingambit', 'Hydrapple', 'Jolteon']
- Weaknesses: The team is weak to fast setup sweepers and strong Ground- or Rock-type pressure, particularly on Moltres, which has a double Rock weakness. Hazards chip Moltres and Jolteon heavily, and few members can force progress against bulky Steel or Water types that wall several attackers. Several members share Fire and Poison coverage, so the team is left short of ways to break through specially defensive Pokemon.
- How to play against: Keep hazards up and pressure Moltres with Rock- and Ground-type attacks. Use a strong Steel- or Water-type to wall Hydrapple and Jolteon, and preserve a Fighting-type or other Kingambit check for late game. Phaze or revenge-kill setup attempts before Nasty Plot, Calm Mind or Swords Dance stack up.

**B. Balance**  (tags: ['balance', 'volturn', 'setup'])

- Game plan: Control hazards and physical threats using defensive pivots Great Tusk and Flame Body Moltres while absorbing special attacks with Assault Vest Slowking-Galar. Generate momentum and chip through Jolteon's Volt Switch and entry hazards set by Great Tusk. Position setup win conditions like Nasty Plot Hydrapple, Calm Mind Jolteon, or Supreme Overlord Kingambit to overwhelm weakened opposing cores in the endgame.
- Roles: Great Tusk: Physical wall, entry hazard setter, and spinner; Hydrapple: Bulky special setup sweeper; Jolteon: Fast pivot and secondary special setup sweeper; Kingambit: Late-game physical setup cleaner; Moltres: Physical tank and status spreader; Slowking-Galar: Specially defensive pivot and coverage attacker
- Win conditions: ['Hydrapple', 'Jolteon', 'Kingambit']
- Weaknesses: Strong Ground- and Water-type wallbreakers can heavily pressure the defensive backbone, particularly if Great Tusk and Moltres take chip damage. The team relies on Assault Vest Slowking-Galar as its primary special buffer without passive recovery, making it vulnerable to strong repeated special hits. Additionally, opposing Knock Off users can strip crucial Heavy-Duty Boots and Leftovers from the team's defensive core.
- How to play against: Apply aggressive offensive pressure using powerful wallbreakers like Kyurem, Darkrai, or Walking Wake that force Slowking-Galar to take continuous chip without recovery. Use Knock Off liberally against Moltres, Hydrapple, and Jolteon to make them susceptible to stealth rock and chip damage. Prevent Kingambit from easily setting up by preserving a healthy Ground- or Fighting-type check into the late game.

**C. Bulky Offense**  (tags: bulky_offense, balance, volturn, setup)

- Game plan: Great Tusk sets Stealth Rock and can use Rapid Spin, with Rocky Helmet and Protosynthesis helping check physical attackers. Moltres and Slowking-Galar form a defensive backbone using Will-O-Wisp/Roost and Assault Vest, while Hydrapple uses Nasty Plot with Giga Drain/Fickle Beam to break. Jolteon provides Volt Switch momentum and a Calm Mind win condition, and Kingambit cleans late with Sucker Punch/Swords Dance under Supreme Overlord.
- Roles: Great Tusk: Stealth Rock setter / Rapid Spin / Rocky Helmet physical check; Hydrapple: Nasty Plot breaker / Giga Drain / Fickle Beam; Jolteon: Volt Switch pivot / Calm Mind / Volt Absorb; Kingambit: Late-game Sucker Punch cleaner / Swords Dance / Supreme Overlord; Moltres: Will-O-Wisp / Roost / Flame Body physical wall; Slowking-Galar: Assault Vest special wall / Sludge Bomb / Psyshock / Flamethrower / Ice Beam
- Win conditions: ['Kingambit', 'Hydrapple', 'Jolteon']
- Weaknesses: The core can be worn down by Knock Off, which removes Assault Vest from Slowking-Galar, Leftovers from Kingambit, and Heavy-Duty Boots from Hydrapple/Jolteon/Moltres, while Great Tusk lacks Heavy-Duty Boots and is vulnerable to Spikes. Strong special wallbreakers such as Iron Moth or Gholdengo can overwhelm Slowking-Galar and Hydrapple before tera, and Fighting-types or Will-O-Wisp can check Kingambit. It also lacks immediate speed outside Jolteon, so fast revenge killers and priority users can pressure the setup win conditions.
- How to play against: Apply early pressure to Great Tusk to deny Stealth Rock and Rapid Spin, and use Knock Off to strip key items. Double switch or use Ground-types/Water-types to punish Jolteon Volt Switch and Moltres, while preserving a faster revenge killer or priority user for Kingambit. Force Slowking-Galar and Hydrapple to take repeated special hits, and use status or Taunt to stop Calm Mind/Nasty Plot/Swords Dance.

## 核心 6: Araquanid + Gholdengo + Iron Moth + Iron Valiant + Kingambit + Raging Bolt

**A. Sticky Web Hyper Offense**  (tags: sticky_web, hyper_offense, setup, hazard_stack)

- Game plan: Araquanid leads to set Sticky Web, crippling the opponent's grounded speed so the team's Booster Energy and setup sweepers move first. Gholdengo's Good as Gold blocks Defog and spin attempts, keeping the Web up while it threatens with Nasty Plot. Iron Moth, Iron Valiant, Raging Bolt and Kingambit then use their boosting moves and priority to break through and clean up. The core wins by chaining setup sweepers behind the speed control of Sticky Web, with Custap Berry Endeavor on Araquanid as a last-ditch trade.
- Roles: Araquanid: Sticky Web lead and suicide hazard setter; Gholdengo: Hazard-preserving spinblocker and Nasty Plot breaker; Iron Moth: Booster Energy special setup sweeper; Iron Valiant: Calm Mind special sweeper with priority; Kingambit: Swords Dance late-game cleaner with Sucker Punch; Raging Bolt: Bulky Calm Mind special breaker with priority Thunderclap
- Win conditions: ['Iron Valiant', 'Iron Moth', 'Kingambit', 'Raging Bolt', 'Gholdengo']
- Weaknesses: The team leans heavily on Sticky Web staying up, so fast Flying-types, Levitate users and Air Balloon holders ignore the speed drop and can revenge kill freely. Araquanid is frail and often trades itself early, leaving the team without a reliable hazard setter if the Web is removed. Multiple Booster Energy users and Air Balloon holders mean the team is item-dependent and can be worn down by residual damage and priority before setup is complete.
- How to play against: Deny or remove Sticky Web with Magic Bounce, Court Change or a fast Taunt, and pressure Araquanid before it can Endeavor. Use Flying-types, Levitate and Air Balloon Pokemon that ignore the Web, plus strong priority to pick off the frail sweepers before they boost. Preserve a bulky check to Kingambit and Raging Bolt and avoid letting Gholdengo set up Nasty Plot for free.

**B. Sticky Web Paradox Offense**  (tags: ['hyper_offense', 'sticky_web', 'setup'])

- Game plan: Araquanid leads with Sticky Web to cut the opponent's Speed, and Custap Berry lets it act first with Endeavor or Lunge. That gives the Booster Energy and setup attackers room to come in. Gholdengo with Nasty Plot, Iron Valiant with Calm Mind, Raging Bolt with Calm Mind and Kingambit with Swords Dance then snowball through a weakened team. Ghost Tera is common across the team and adds a defensive pivot and extra offensive power.
- Roles: Araquanid: Sticky Web lead and Endeavor revenge; Gholdengo: Nasty Plot special sweeper and Good as Gold status blocker; Iron Moth: Special wallbreaker with Fiery Dance and Sludge Wave; Iron Valiant: Calm Mind setup attacker; Kingambit: Swords Dance late-game cleaner with Sucker Punch priority; Raging Bolt: Calm Mind special sweeper
- Win conditions: ['Raging Bolt', 'Iron Valiant', 'Gholdengo', 'Kingambit']
- Weaknesses: Sticky Web is easy to remove with Defog, Rapid Spin or Magic Bounce, and Araquanid is fragile and loses its role if it is knocked out early. Dark types, Fairy types and fast priority users threaten several members, and Ghost Tera is weak to Dark moves. Heavy reliance on Booster Energy makes the team vulnerable to Unaware users and to phazing.
- How to play against: Remove or bounce back Sticky Web, or lead something that ignores it such as Corviknight or a Ground type with Tera Flying. Use Unaware or phazing to stop Calm Mind and Nasty Plot, and keep priority or Dark attacks ready for the Ghost Teras. Kill Araquanid before it gets the web up.

**C. Sticky Web Hyper Offense**  (tags: ['hyper_offense', 'sticky_web', 'setup'])

- Game plan: Araquanid sets up Sticky Web and uses Custap Berry alongside Endeavor to weaken threats early. Gholdengo blocks hazard removal with Good as Gold while threatening teams with Nasty Plot, Make It Rain, and Shadow Ball. Setup sweepers like Booster Energy Iron Moth, Iron Valiant, Raging Bolt, and Supreme Overlord Kingambit leverage the speed control from webs to overpower opposing teams.
- Roles: Araquanid: Sticky Web lead and Custap Endeavor user; Gholdengo: Spinblocker and Nasty Plot sweeper; Iron Moth: Special setup sweeper; Iron Valiant: Calm Mind wallbreaker and sweeper; Kingambit: Late-game cleaner and physical setup sweeper; Raging Bolt: Calm Mind sweeper and priority revenge killer
- Win conditions: ['Kingambit', 'Iron Moth', 'Iron Valiant', 'Raging Bolt']
- Weaknesses: Heavy-Duty Boots users and naturally airborne or Flying-type Pokémon completely ignore Sticky Web, bypassing the team's primary speed control. Fast priority moves and opposing Booster Energy attackers can disrupt sweepers before they get moving.
- How to play against: Prevent Sticky Web from going up if possible, or deploy Court Change or Rapid Spin when Gholdengo is safely pressured out. Utilize Heavy-Duty Boots attackers and strong priority to pick off boosted sweepers before they snowball.

## 核心 8: Great Tusk + Ninetales + Walking Wake

**A. Sun Hazard Offense with Walking Wake**  (tags: ['sun', 'bulky_offense', 'hazard_stack', 'setup'])

- Game plan: Ninetales sets Drought with Heat Rock, which boosts Fire moves and Protosynthesis. Great Tusk lays Stealth Rock, uses Rapid Spin to clear hazards, and pressures with Headlong Rush and Knock Off. Walking Wake hits hard with Hydro Steam, which is boosted in sun, along with Draco Meteor and Flip Turn pivoting. Ninetales can also support the team with Will-O-Wisp, Encore, Healing Wish and Scorching Sands.
- Roles: Great Tusk: Hazard setter, spinner and physical attacker; Ninetales: Sun setter and utility support; Walking Wake: Special wallbreaker and pivot
- Win conditions: ['Walking Wake', 'Great Tusk']
- Weaknesses: Ninetales is frail and weak to Stealth Rock, and it loses its sun if it is removed or outlasted. Great Tusk and Walking Wake both struggle against bulky Water-types and Fairy-types such as Gliscor-style Ground absorbers or Dondozo-style walls. The team also has only one real sun setter, so it has few ways to reset the weather.
- How to play against: Pressure Ninetales early with hazards, fast attackers or Rock and Water moves to end the sun. Use bulky Fairy- or Steel-types to wall Walking Wake's Draco Meteor, and Flying-types or Ground-resistant Pokemon to blunt Great Tusk. Rain or other weather changes also weaken the Fire and Protosynthesis synergy.

**B. Sun Bulky Offense**  (tags: ['sun', 'bulky_offense', 'setup'])

- Game plan: Ninetales sets extended sunlight via Drought and Heat Rock, using Encore and Healing Wish to support its teammates and bring sweepers in safely. Under the sun, Walking Wake fires off massive Life Orb or Choice Specs boosted Hydro Steam and Draco Meteor attacks, utilizing Protosynthesis to outspeed or overpower defensive structures. Great Tusk ensures rapid hazard removal with Rapid Spin and entry hazard control with Stealth Rock, while also acting as an offensive physical threat with Bulk Up or Headlong Rush.
- Roles: Great Tusk: Hazard control and physical wallbreaker; Ninetales: Sun setter and utility pivot; Walking Wake: Special wallbreaker and sun sweeper
- Win conditions: ['Walking Wake', 'Great Tusk']
- Weaknesses: Opposing weather setters like Pelipper disrupt the core by clearing sun and weakening Walking Wake's Hydro Steam and Great Tusk's Protosynthesis boosts. Specially defensive walls like Blissey or Clodsire can absorb Walking Wake's onslaught, while fast Fairy- and Dragon-type attackers can heavily pressure both Walking Wake and Great Tusk.
- How to play against: Contest weather actively by preserving manual weather setters or utilizing strong priority moves to handle weakened offensive threats. Target Ninetales early to prevent it from setting Sun repeatedly or offering Healing Wish recovery, and pressure Great Tusk with special attacks to limit its hazard clearing.

**C. Sun Offense**  (tags: sun, bulky_offense, hazard_stack, volturn, setup)

- Game plan: Ninetales sets sun with Drought and Heat Rock, enabling Protosynthesis on Great Tusk and Walking Wake. Great Tusk sets Stealth Rock, removes hazards with Rapid Spin, and pressures with Headlong Rush, Ice Spinner, Knock Off, or Bulk Up. Walking Wake abuses sun-boosted Hydro Steam and Protosynthesis as a special breaker, using Flip Turn to keep momentum or Draco Meteor to punch through. Ninetales supports with Encore, Will-O-Wisp, and Healing Wish to create free turns for its teammates.
- Roles: Great Tusk: hazard setter/remover and physical breaker; Ninetales: sun setter and support; Walking Wake: sun-boosted special wallbreaker
- Win conditions: ['Walking Wake', 'Great Tusk']
- Weaknesses: The core depends heavily on Ninetales keeping sun up; opposing weather setters such as Pelipper, Tyranitar, and Alolan Ninetales can overwrite Drought, and Ninetales is frail enough to be forced out or worn down by Stealth Rock and Knock Off. Walking Wake can be checked by bulky Fairy- and Water-types and revenge killed by faster Dragon- or Fairy-type attackers, while Great Tusk can be worn down by Will-O-Wisp and Flying-, Grass-, or Ice-type coverage. Losing Heat Rock or failing to keep hazards off the field significantly reduces the core's offensive pressure.
- How to play against: Pressure Ninetales immediately with Rock-, Ground-, or Water-type attacks and Knock Off to remove Heat Rock, then change the weather with your own weather setter to deny Protosynthesis and sun-boosted Hydro Steam. Use specially bulky Fairy- or Water-types to absorb Walking Wake's attacks and revenge kill it with faster Dragon- or Fairy-types, while punishing Great Tusk with Flying-, Grass-, or Ice-type moves and avoiding giving it free Rapid Spin or Stealth Rock turns. Preserve your weather setter and priority users so the sun core cannot freely cycle Healing Wish and Flip Turn momentum.
